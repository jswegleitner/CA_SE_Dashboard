# California Board (BPELS / BPELSG) statistics on structural engineer exam results, California candidates, 2004-2026

Researched 2026-11-13. All figures are California candidates unless a row says otherwise. "calc." marks a percentage I computed from the Board's printed tested/passed counts because the Board printed no percentage; "derived" marks a figure obtained by subtracting one Board figure from another. Nothing is interpolated.

Short names for the sources used repeatedly (full list with saved filenames is under the last key question):

- **Archived per-administration pages**: `https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/<page>.shtml` (the live pages now return 404).
- **Archived index (2025)**: https://web.archive.org/web/20250222120857/https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/exam_statistics.shtml (one long page holding every administration from 1999 to Fall 2017).
- **Board meeting materials**: `https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/<yyyymmdd>_meeting_materials.pdf`.
- **Bulletins**: `https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/pubs/bulletin<NN>.pdf`.

## What does the Board's exam statistics page report for the Structural Engineer exams for each administration?

### Takeaway
The Board published California tested/passed counts for the structural exams on its Examination Statistics web page for every administration from October 2004 through Spring 2017, then stopped (the Fall 2017 entry and the current PDF carry state-specific exams only). From 2018 on the same California counts appear only in Board meeting packets and the spring "exam results" Bulletin, which I used to fill 2018-2026; Fall 2017 is the one administration with no California figure anywhere I looked.

### Cited Findings
- The current page is a PDF, "Examination Statistics (State Specific and ASBOG Examinations)", revised August 2026. It covers Civil Seismic, Civil Surveying, Land Surveyor, Traffic, Geotechnical and geology exams from 2019 to Q2/Spring 2026 and has **no structural engineer rows**; it says "NCEES examination statistics are available on the NCEES website". The old `.shtml` address now returns a "300 Multiple Choices" page pointing to the PDF. — [BPELSG Examination Statistics PDF](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/exam_statistics.pdf)
- The Wayback Machine holds the old page under both domains: `pels.ca.gov/applicants/exam_statistics.shtml` (captures 2008-04 to 2012-06) and `bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/exam_statistics.shtml` (captures 2012-08 to 2025-02). Until about 2017 it was a list of links to one page per administration (`oct04stats.shtml`, `ap06stats.shtml`, ... `spring14stats.shtml`, `oct14stats.shtml`, `spring15stats.shtml`, `fall2015stats.shtml`, `spring16stats.shtml`, `fall2016stats.shtml`); from 2018 it was a single page with collapsible sections. — [2010 capture, pels.ca.gov](https://web.archive.org/web/20100729114200/http://www.pels.ca.gov/applicants/exam_statistics.shtml); [2017 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20170303051143/http://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/exam_statistics.shtml); [Archived index (2025)](https://web.archive.org/web/20250222120857/https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/exam_statistics.shtml)
- The page's last structural entry is Spring 2017. Its "Fall 2017 Exam Statistics" section lists only state exams and says NCEES results "can be found on the NCEES website". Captures from 2020, 2022 and 2025 are unchanged, so nothing later was ever added to the page. — [Archived index (2025)](https://web.archive.org/web/20250222120857/https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/exam_statistics.shtml)
- The April 2004 and April 2005 pages list no structural exam at all (April 2004 lists Civil, Land Surveying, Chemical, Electrical, Mechanical only). The first April structural row is April 2006. — [April 2004 page](https://web.archive.org/web/20160202214616/http://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/ap04stats.shtml); [April 2005 page](https://web.archive.org/web/20160202214621/http://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/ap05stats.shtml); [April 2006 page](https://web.archive.org/web/20160202214627/http://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/ap06stats.shtml)
- The pages do not say whether counts are first-time or repeat takers; every figure is all California examinees for that exam at that administration. The one "1st Time vs. Repeat Test Takers" page (`multistats.shtml`) sits among the 2001-2002 links and has no structural content. — [multistats page](https://web.archive.org/web/20151110011348/http://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/multistats.shtml)

### Inferences
- The absence of April 2004 and April 2005 structural rows most likely means California offered both structural exams only in October in those years (the October 2003 entry on the index is a single "Structural" exam, 290 tested, 73 passed, 25.17%, cutscore 650 of 1500, i.e. the older all-California format). The Board page does not state this.
- The Board's own row labels are "National Structural" (the NCEES exam) and "State-Specific SE" / "State-Specific Structural" (the California exam, SE III). The pages never use the names "Structural II" or "SE III".

### Gaps
- Fall 2017 (October 2017) California results: not on the page, not in a Bulletin, and not found in the January-May 2018 meeting packets.
- I did not fetch captures of `dca.ca.gov/pels` (the Wayback index query for it timed out). Not needed for 2004 onward, since the 2016 captures of the bpelsg.ca.gov pages cover October 2004 forward.

## What were California pass rates on SE III and on NCEES Structural II, each administration 2004-2010?

The California-written exam ran once a year in October and passed 27-36% of California examinees in every year except October 2009 (50.43%); it produced 83-177 passers a year. The NCEES national exam was offered in October (176-279 tested, 33-51% passing) and, from 2006, also in April with far fewer takers (67-91 tested, 23-38% passing).

### Inferences
- "State-Specific SE" is the California-written SE III and "National Structural" in 2004-2010 is NCEES Structural II; the Board pages do not name them, so this mapping rests on the task description and the era, not on the page text.
- A candidate needed both exams, and the Board never published how many held both, so the number of people completing the requirement each year cannot be read from this table. The state exam's passer count (83-177 a year) is the tighter of the two constraints in most years.
- The October 2011 state exam (132 tested, 36 passed) shows California gave SE III one more time after the 16-hour exam began in April 2011, presumably for candidates finishing under the old rules. The "National Structural" rows for April and October 2011 are not split into Vertical and Lateral, so it is unclear what population they cover (possible readings: candidates sitting the full 16-hour exam, or a combined count across components). Treat the 2011 national rows as not comparable with the rows on either side.

### Gaps
- April 2004 and April 2005: no structural rows published (see first question).
- No first-time versus repeat split exists for any structural exam in this era.
- The Board published no count of candidates who completed both exams.

### Inferences
- **Fall 2015 Lateral should be 98 tested, not 68.** The 2018 sunset report's FY 2015/16 Lateral rate of 42% matches (31+63)/(98+126) = 42.0%; with 68 it would be 48%. Bulletin 50's "37 unacceptable" is most likely a misprint for 67.
- **The fiscal-year table checks out against the per-administration data for FY 2014/15 through FY 2018/19** (e.g. FY 2018/19 Lateral 244 = Fall 2018's 115 + April 2019's 129; Vertical 248 = 123 + 125; FY 2014/15 Lateral (57+45)/(143+101) = 41.8%). That confirms a fiscal year is the October sitting plus the next April sitting.
- **October 2019 can therefore be read from the FY 2019/20 row**, because April 2020 was cancelled: Lateral 100 candidates at 31% (about 31 passers), Vertical 116 at 34% (about 39 passers). The passer counts are my arithmetic, not printed figures.
- **The FY 2020/21 to FY 2022/23 rows do not reconcile with the per-administration counts.** Fall 2020 + Spring 2021 gives Lateral 197 and Vertical 166, against the report's 190 and 163; Fall 2021 + Spring 2022 gives 241 and 247 against 210 and 217; derived Fall 2022 + Spring 2023 gives 273 and 273 against 225 and 212. The sunset table may count a different population in those years (for example by application date or excluding some candidates). Prefer the per-administration figures for charting and treat the later fiscal-year rows as approximate.
- **Fall 2017 is bounded but not known.** FY 2017/18 was 27% Lateral and 35% Vertical overall and April 2018 alone was 21% and 31%, so Fall 2017 must have been above 27% Lateral and above 35% Vertical. No counts can be derived.
- Passers per component per year in California were about 110-150 through 2013, about 75-105 from 2014 to 2019, and 58-95 in 2021-2023. Because a candidate needs both components, annual completions are bounded above by the smaller of the two component passer counts in the relevant window, which fell to 58 (Vertical) in 2023.

### Gaps
- **How many California candidates acquired both components: not published anywhere I found.** The web page, Bulletins, meeting packets and both sunset reports give component-level results only.
- Fall 2017 California counts and rates.
- October 2019 passer counts (only candidates and percentage, via the fiscal-year row).
- No first-time versus repeat split, and no buildings versus bridges split, for California in any Board document.
- April and October 2011 component-level (Vertical/Lateral) results: the Board printed one combined "National Structural" row.

## What do sunset reports, annual reports, minutes, agenda packets and Executive Officer reports say about SE exam pass rates, candidate volumes, and concern about low pass rates or the 2024 computer-based transition?

### Takeaway
The Board's formal reports treat the structural exam as a routine national exam and report only component pass rates; explicit concern appears in the August 2024 minutes, where the Executive Officer called sub-20% pass rates "a cause for concern" and SEAOC and a practising SE criticised the computer-based exam. Follow-up items on NCEES's response appear in 2025 packets.

### Cited Findings
- August 22, 2024 minutes, Executive Officer Ric Moore: "this spring was the first time that the National Structural Engineer Exam was administered via Computer Based Testing (CBT)"; "any time there is a change in format, there will be fluctuation in results"; the structural engineering community "has expressed concern with the current pass rates especially in the depth portion of the exam"; there was "a higher incidence of repeat test takers which will ultimately affect the pass rate"; he "believes California candidates did as well or even better than the average population. However, when you see pass rates below 20%, there is a cause for concern." — [Board minutes 2024-08-22](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/minutes/20240822_bdmn.pdf)
- Same meeting, public comment by Carl Josephson (SEAOC): SEAOC had taken no formal position; nationally there was "about a 50% pass rate for multiple choice" and the pass rate for the Alternate Item Type questions that replaced the pencil-and-paper portion "was around the 15% range"; "California did slightly better than the national average"; "20% is still low. An ideal position for a pass rate is between 30% and 50%"; "California makes up about 30% of the exams purchased from NCEES"; the exam is "a 21-hour exam over four days"; "One third of the questions on the exam are not graded." — [Board minutes 2024-08-22](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/minutes/20240822_bdmn.pdf)
- Same meeting, public comment by Krista Looza, SE (Buehler Engineering): the cost to sit the SE exam "has increased by approximately 800% in the last 12 years"; she argued standards had fallen since California administered its own exam, did not believe NCEES could correct the issues with the structural exam and CBT, and asked the Board to act. — [Board minutes 2024-08-22](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/minutes/20240822_bdmn.pdf)
- The May 29, 2025 packet's report on the NCEES Western Zone meeting has a section headed "Structural Exam Administration Issues": discussion centred on the transition of the PE-Structural exam to CBT "(which began April 2024)" and administration troubles, and "some boards were concerned with the low pass rates in comparison to pass rates from recent history prior to the transition." A Pearson VUE evaluation "identified a 'speededness' associated with the Depth exam sections", meaning all examinees were experiencing a time issue, leading to "an additional one hour, for a total time of 6.5 hours" on the Depth sections "beginning in October 2025". A Washington Board motion to cut the number of Depth questions immediately passed in two zone sessions but the NCEES Board of Directors was reported to have denied it. A new job-analysis study (PAKS) for the exam was nearing completion. — [Meeting materials 2025-05-29](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20250529_meeting_materials.pdf)
- Minutes reproduced in the May 14, 2026 packet: Carl Josephson (SEAOC) "suggested including test results for all four of the engineering exam components, breadth and depth, vertical and lateral", said depth pass rates had been low nationally, and noted that candidates get an additional hour for the depth portion "while maintaining its current 60 questions" and that "Beginning next year, the depth portion will retain the extended time but will reduce the total question count to 48"; he "advised that these changes may cause pass rates to fluctuate". — [Meeting materials 2026-05-14](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20260514_meeting_materials.pdf)
- 2023-24 Sunset Review Report: "The final national examination to transition to CBT is the PE Structural Engineer examination, formerly known as the 16-hour Structural Engineering exam, scheduled to begin in spring 2024." Its Table 8 gives component candidates and pass rates for FY 2018/19-2022/23 (table in the previous question) and lists the last NCEES occupational analysis as 2018. — [2023-24 Sunset Review Report](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/pubs/2023-24_sunset_review_report.pdf)
- 2018 Sunset Review Report: Table 8 gives component pass rates only for FY 2014/15-2017/18 (Lateral 42%, 42%, 36%, 27%; Vertical 42%, 40%, 40%, 35%), with no candidate counts and no commentary on the decline. — [2018 Sunset Review Report, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20181130022053/https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/pubs/2018_sunset_review_report.pdf)
- Fall 2023 Bulletin: registration for the CBT PE Structural exam opened November 1, 2023, and California applicants "may now register and sit for the CBT PE Structural Exam without" first applying to the Board; once a candidate has passed both components, has the qualifying experience and holds an active California Civil Engineer licence, they may apply for SE licensure, with applications accepted continuously. — [Bulletin 74](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/pubs/bulletin74.pdf)
- Spring 2022 Bulletin lead article by Board member Dr. Alireza Asgari, SE, explains the planned change: Vertical and Lateral components each split into a breadth section and a depth section, with depth using alternative item types, and under the 16-hour format a failed component had to be retaken whole. — [Bulletin 69](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/pubs/bulletin69.pdf)
- Minutes reproduced in the March 2020 packet: Carl Josephson (SEAOC) reported "a lot of discussion regarding NCEES's plans to convert the structural exam to computer-based testing by 2024" and that he knew of no discussion of making it less than a 16-hour exam with two 8-hour components. — [Meeting materials 2020-03-12](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20200312_meeting_materials.pdf)
- The Board's February 2024 SE exam FAQ sets the acceptable combinations: both components on the CBT exam, both on the 16-hour exam, or one from each. — BPELSG SE Exam FAQ (already saved in the project as `research/sources/BPELSG_SE_Exam_FAQ_2024-02.pdf`)

### Inferences
- The Board accepts a mix of 16-hour and CBT component passes, so candidates holding one 16-hour component in 2023 could finish with a single CBT component (two sections) rather than four sections. That cohort, plus comity applicants, likely accounts for much of 2024-2025 licensing.
- The 21% Lateral result in April 2018 and the 20-25% Vertical results in 2023 drew no recorded comment in the documents I read; recorded concern starts only with the 2024 computer-based results.
- The Vertical Depth jump from 12% (April 2025) to 29% (October 2025) for California coincides with the extra hour added to depth sections from October 2025. One sitting is not enough to attribute the change, and a further format change (48 questions) follows in 2026 or 2027 depending on how "next year" in those minutes is dated.

### Gaps
- I did not read every packet and set of minutes from 2018-2026 in full; I searched their text for structural exam tables and for the word "structural". Discussion that does not use that word would be missed.
- The 2010-11 and 2014-15 Board sunset review reports were not located (no Wayback capture found by filename; the Wayback index went offline during the search). Only the 2015 and 2019 legislative background papers, already in the project, were available, and neither prints SE exam pass rates.
- DCA annual reports were not opened (see next question).
