# NCEES national pass rates: Structural I / Structural II (1985-2010) and the 16-hour SE exam (April 2011 - October 2023)

Research date: 2026-11-13. All figures are NATIONAL NCEES figures. "FT" = first-time takers, "Rpt" = repeat takers.

How the numbers were read: every NCEES PDF below was downloaded from ncees.org and read with `pdftotext` (both `-layout` and `-raw` modes, cross-checked). No PDF page renderer was available, so nothing was checked by eye against the printed page. Where text extraction was ambiguous it is flagged. Nothing is interpolated.

Source labels used in the tables:
- **LE** = NCEES *Licensure Exchange* newsletter (primary; prints the pass rates for one administration).
- **SQ** = NCEES *Squared* annual statistics report (primary; prints volumes and pass rates for a whole NCEES fiscal year, 1 October - 30 September, i.e. normally one October plus the following April administration combined).
- **WSG** = William S. Godfrey's "NCEES SE Exam Historical Pass Rates" page (SECONDARY; a private engineer's running log, "obtained directly from NCEES unless otherwise noted", last updated 20 January 2025).

## Key question 1: First-time and repeat pass rates for each SE exam component, April 2011 - October 2023

### Takeaway
A complete administration-by-administration series exists for **Vertical** and **Lateral** (buildings from October 2014 on) for all 25 administrations from October 2011 to October 2023, but only 5 of the 25 are confirmed directly from an NCEES document (Oct 2011, Apr 2012, Oct 2013, Oct 2019, Oct 2023); the other 20 rest on one secondary compilation (WSG) that agrees with NCEES everywhere it can be checked except one fiscal year. Bridge-component rates per administration were found only for October 2019 and October 2023 (and April 2015 via a search-engine snippet); otherwise bridges exist only as NCEES fiscal-year totals for FY2016-FY2023. April 2011 has no component rates at all: NCEES published only a 27% both-components rate and declined to publish component statistics.

**Disagreement to record**

### Inferences
- The mapping of each Squared edition to "October of the prior year + April of the report year" is my inference from each report's stated fiscal year (1 Oct - 30 Sep). It is supported by three exact checks: Squared 2020 volumes are about half a normal year and NCEES says April 2020 was cancelled; Squared 2024's pencil-and-paper rows equal WSG's October 2023 row exactly; Squared 2020's buildings rows equal WSG's October 2019 row exactly.
- WSG is a credible gap-filler: it matches NCEES exactly on all 5 administrations that can be checked directly, and its paired administrations are arithmetically consistent with the Squared fiscal-year totals for FY2016, FY2018, FY2019, FY2021, FY2022 and FY2023 (first-time and repeat, vertical and lateral buildings). The one exception is the FY2017 vertical repeat figure above; one of WSG's two repeat values for Oct 2016 / Apr 2017 is probably wrong, but which one cannot be determined from what I found.
- For a chart, the buildings series is the national series that matters by volume: bridges volumes are roughly 10-15% of buildings volumes (e.g. FY2023: 97 vs 654 first-time vertical takers).
- April 2011's 27% is a different statistic (passed both components in one sitting) and should not be plotted on the same line as the per-component rates.

### Gaps
- No NCEES-published per-administration figures were found for 20 of 25 administrations (Oct 2012, Apr 2013, Apr 2014 through Apr 2019 except as fiscal-year totals, Oct 2020 through Apr 2023). They exist only in WSG. The Wayback Machine snapshots of the NCEES pass-rate pages that would confirm them could not be fetched (see Blocked sources).
- Bridges per-administration rates: found only for Oct 2019 and Oct 2023 (NCEES) and Apr 2015 (snippet). For all other administrations from Oct 2014 on, bridges are available only as fiscal-year totals (FY2016-FY2023). No bridges data of any kind for Oct 2014 and FY2014-15 (Squared 2015 not found).
- Component rates for April 2011 were never published by NCEES as far as I can find (NCEES's exam director said component stats "aren't relevant").
- Squared 2014 (the inaugural issue, FY2013-14) and Squared 2015 are no longer on ncees.org; I could not locate either PDF.
- The engineerboards thread contains an image ("NCEES-SE-Statistics.jpg") of historical statistics whose contents are not in the page text.

**Other findings**
- Structural II changed format in April 2004. Before April 2004 it "consisted of a choice between one four-hour building and one four-hour bridge problem offered in both the morning and afternoon sessions"; the new format was meant to test "the same core knowledge in the same way" each administration, and "the Structural II Exam Committee anticipates that these format changes will bring more consistency to the pass rates." — [Licensure Exchange Dec 2003](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/le_2003_12.pdf)
- Structural I and II were administered for the last time in October 2010 and replaced by the 16-hour exam on 8-9 April 2011. — [Licensure Exchange Aug 2011, p.13](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Licensure-Exchange_Licensure-Exchange-August-2011.pdf)
- Secondary and unverified: a web-search summary of an eng-tips thread reports April 2004 rates of 45% (Structural I) and 49% (Structural II) for first-time takers and 8% and 51% for repeat takers. I could not open the thread (403), so the pairing of numbers to exams is not confirmed. — [eng-tips.com thread 97363](https://www.eng-tips.com/threads/april-2004-stuct-i-pe-takers.97363)

### Inferences
- The April 2004 format change shows clearly in the national numbers: those sitting both sessions of the old Structural II passed at 31%, 14% and 15% (Apr 2001 - Apr 2002), against 51-65% first-time on the new format (Oct 2004 - Oct 2010). Anyone comparing California SE license counts before and after 2004 should treat the two Structural II eras as different exams.
- In 2004-2010 Structural II (the exam California used) consistently had a higher first-time rate than Structural I (51-65% vs 38-48%), likely because its takers had already passed Structural I or held a PE; this is my reading, not an NCEES statement.
- April 2009 and October 2009 both show Structural II at exactly 65 / 36. Both were read from separate issues with their own headings, so I record them as printed, but identical consecutive values are worth a second look against the PDFs.

### Gaps
- 1985-2000: no national Structural I / Structural II pass rates found. The Licensure Exchange archive on ncees.org starts with 2001 issues.
- October 2002, April 2003, October 2003, April 2004 and April 2005: no primary figures. The February 2003, August 2004 and August 2005 Licensure Exchange post pages exist on ncees.org but have no PDF link; the 2003-2004 issues that do have PDFs print no pass-rate table.
- No examinee counts for Structural I / II after April 2002 (Structural I: 567 in April 2001 is the only Structural I count found).
- Structural II repeat-taker rates before October 2004 were not published separately in the issues found.

## Key question 3: How many examinees sat each exam/component per year

### Takeaway
NCEES Squared gives first-time and repeat volumes for all four SE components for fiscal years 2015-16 through 2023-24; in a normal two-administration year about 1,000-1,100 people sat each buildings component and about 110-190 sat each bridges component. Earlier volumes are sparse: a handful of 2001-2002 counts and the April 2015 administration.

### Cited Findings
- Fiscal-year volumes for every component, first-time and repeat, FY2016-FY2024: see Table 1C above (first number in each cell). Example totals (FT + Rpt): Vertical Buildings 938 (FY2016), 1,064 (FY2017), 1,055 (FY2018), 1,065 (FY2019), 473 (FY2020, October 2019 only), 1,060 (FY2021), 964 (FY2022), 1,038 (FY2023), 478 (FY2024, October 2023 only). These sums are my arithmetic on the Squared cells. — [Squared 2016](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/Squared-2016.pdf), [2017](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/Squared-2017-web.pdf), [2018](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/Squared-2018-for-web.pdf), [2019](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Squared-2019_web.pdf), [2020](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Squared-2020_spreads_web.pdf), [2021](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Squared-2021_web.pdf), [2022](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Squared-2022-new-3-1.pdf), [2023](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Squared-2023_web-1.pdf), [2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Squared-2024_pages.pdf)
- Squared 2018's exam-volume summary lists the SE exam at 2,528 pencil-and-paper examinees for FY2017-18 (0 CBT). This is the sum of component sittings, so a person taking both components counts twice (the eight overall-taker cells for FY2018 in Table 1C add to 2,531, within 3 of that figure). — [NCEES Squared 2018](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/Squared-2018-for-web.pdf)
- Single-administration volumes: April 2015, October 2019 and October 2023 by component in Table 1B.
- First administration, April 2011: "Over 80 percent of them took Vertical Forces on Friday and then came back for Lateral Forces on Saturday"; 49 of 54 jurisdictions offered the exam. No headcount given. — [Licensure Exchange Aug 2011, p.13](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Licensure-Exchange_Licensure-Exchange-August-2011.pdf)
- Structural I: 567 examinees in April 2001. Structural II: 169 sat both portions in April 2001 (plus 49 morning-only and 49 afternoon-only); 322 took some portion in October 2001; 310 in April 2002. — [Licensure Exchange Aug 2001](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Aug-2001-LEx.pdf), [Feb 2002](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Feb-2002-LEx.pdf), [Aug 2002](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Aug-2002-LEx.pdf)

### Inferences
- Repeat takers are a large share of every sitting (e.g. FY2023 Lateral Buildings: 529 first-time vs 508 repeat), so a combined pass rate for any administration sits well below the first-time rate usually quoted.
- Squared volumes count component sittings, not unique people; the number of distinct candidates per year is lower and is not published in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No volumes for the SE exam for April 2011 - April 2014, October 2014, or FY2014-15 (Squared 2014 and 2015 not found; NCEES's web pages showed volumes from October 2014 but the archived pages could not be fetched).
- No volumes for Structural I / II for 2002-2010, and none for 1985-2000.
- No count of unique SE examinees or of people who completed both components in any year.

## Key question 4: Scoring method, and commentary on why SE pass rates were low

### Takeaway
NCEES documents describe a criterion-referenced approach in practice: a standard-setting ("cut-score") panel of licensed structural engineers fixed the passing standard for the 16-hour exam in June 2011, intended as a baseline for several years, with essay answers double-graded against set criteria. I found no NCEES statement of the year criterion-referenced scoring was first introduced, and no NCEES explanation of why SE pass rates are low; available commentary on that is forum-level only.

### Cited Findings
- 16-hour exam scoring: morning sessions are machine-scored multiple choice; afternoon sessions are constructed-response questions "graded by teams of subject-matter experts". A scoring workshop on 9-11 June 2011 used 85 licensed structural engineers; "each response was graded by two subject-matter experts, with a third used if necessary." — [Licensure Exchange Aug 2011, p.13](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Licensure-Exchange_Licensure-Exchange-August-2011.pdf)
- Cut score: "NCEES invited structural engineers to participate in a cut-score, or standard-setting, study. About 45 structural engineers met in Clemson on June 17-18 to not only help determine the passing score for the April 2011 administration but also to set a baseline for passing scores for the next several years." — [Licensure Exchange Aug 2011, p.13](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Licensure-Exchange_Licensure-Exchange-August-2011.pdf)
- Passing rule: examinees "had to demonstrate minimum competency in both the morning and afternoon session of each component", and needed acceptable results on both components to pass the exam. — [Licensure Exchange Aug 2011, p.13](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Licensure-Exchange_Licensure-Exchange-August-2011.pdf)
- NCEES on reporting: Tim Miller, director of exam services: "Stats for the separate components aren't relevant. You need both to pass the exam, so we want to keep the focus on the exam as a whole." (NCEES reversed this by February 2012, when it began printing component rates.) — [Licensure Exchange Aug 2011, p.13](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Licensure-Exchange_Licensure-Exchange-August-2011.pdf); component rates printed in [Licensure Exchange Feb 2012, p.9](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Licensure-Exchange_Licensure-Exchange-February-2012.pdf)
- Structural I vs II scoring (2003): "The Structural I exam is 100% multiple choice and machine scored... The Structural II exam is composed of essay problems designed to measure the ability to integrate structural knowledge... The Structural II exam is scored in a workshop environment" with a coordinator per problem working from a written solution and scoring criteria. Content came from the 2000 Professional Activities and Knowledge Study. — [Licensure Exchange Dec 2003](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/le_2003_12.pdf)
- General NCEES cut-score practice as described by an exam-prep provider (secondary): panels of practitioners estimate expected performance and recommend a cut score; NCEES repeats the exercise roughly every eight years and does not publish the passing score. This came from a search summary of the page, not my own reading of it. — [School of PE, "How Does the NCEES Exam Scoring Process Work?"](https://www.schoolofpe.com/blog/2022/05/how-does-ncees-exam-scoring-process-work.html)
- Forum commentary (secondary, anecdotal): an engineerboards poster computed averages through October 2014 of first-time vertical 46.1% (+/-3.8), first-time lateral 38.3% (+/-6.8), repeat vertical 30.3% (+/-4.1), repeat lateral 33.4% (+/-10.0); another recalled a first-time rate of "about 16%" when "the first structural exam came out" around 2004-2005 (a recollection, not data, and not consistent with the 56% NCEES printed for Structural II in October 2004). — [engineerboards.com thread 24717](https://engineerboards.com/threads/historical-se-exam-pass-rates.24717/)

### Inferences
- The lateral component was consistently the harder one for first-time takers (WSG mean 35% vs 44% vertical), and lateral rates swing more between administrations (25% in Oct 2012, 50% in Apr 2016). With a fixed cut score, swings of that size point to variation in exam difficulty or the candidate pool, but no source says which.
- The need to pass two separately-scored 8-hour components, each requiring minimum competency in both a multiple-choice and an essay session, mechanically pushes the complete-exam pass rate below either component rate; the only whole-exam figure NCEES ever printed is 27% (April 2011).

### Gaps
- No source found stating when NCEES adopted criterion-referenced scoring for the structural exams, or using the phrase "criterion-referenced" for them.
- No NCEES, NCSEA, SEAOC or STRUCTURE magazine explanation of low SE pass rates was retrieved. Two STRUCTURE magazine PDFs that search results pointed to return 404 (see Blocked sources).
- NCEES never published the cut scores themselves.
