# Computer-based NCEES PE Structural exam: pass rates and professional reaction, April 2024 to November 2026

Research date: 2026-11-13. All figures are national (NCEES member-board examinees) unless marked California or Washington. "First-time" and "repeat" are NCEES's own categories. Nothing below is interpolated; where a window was not found it is listed under Gaps.

Reading note on PDF tables: several NCEES/BPELSG tables were read from `pdftotext -layout` output, where row labels can drift by a line. Where I had to re-align a row label I say so. The saved PDFs (listed at the end) should be opened to confirm before any figure is published.

## 2. How do those compare with the final pencil-and-paper administrations (2022-2023)?

### Takeaway
On the paper 16-hour exam, buildings first-time pass rates were roughly 26-39% per component; the CBT buildings depth sections came in at 13-20% through April 2025, about half or less. Bridges results were comparable or better under CBT. The two formats are not like-for-like: paper used compensatory scoring across multiple-choice and essay portions of one component, CBT requires four separate passes.

### Cited Findings
- Paper exam, fiscal year 1 Oct 2022 - 30 Sep 2023 (October 2022 and April 2023 administrations), overall takers: Lateral Forces Bridges first-time 71 at 27%, repeat 70 at 31%; Lateral Forces Buildings first-time 529 at 31%, repeat 508 at 31%; Vertical Forces Bridges first-time 97 at 44%, repeat 59 at 51%; Vertical Forces Buildings first-time 654 at 39%, repeat 384 at 30% — [NCEES Squared 2023](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Squared-2023_spreads.pdf).
- Paper exam, final administration October 2023 (the only paper window in fiscal 2023-24): Lateral Forces Bridges first-time 34 at 29%, repeat 49 at 18%; Lateral Forces Buildings first-time 271 at 39%, repeat 310 at 48%; Vertical Forces Bridges first-time 31 at 55%, repeat 33 at 30%; Vertical Forces Buildings first-time 241 at 26%, repeat 237 at 25% — [NCEES Squared 2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Squared-2024_pages.pdf). (The Vertical Forces Bridges numbers sit on a displaced line in the extraction; confirm in the PDF.)
- Structural difference: the paper exam was two 8-hour components with a "hybrid compensatory grading system" in which a weak essay portion could be offset by a strong multiple-choice portion; the CBT exam is four separately graded sections (21 hours over at least four days) and "acceptable results are required for each" — [Josephson, STRUCTURE, Sept 2024](https://www.structuremag.org/article/what-in-the-world-is-going-on-with-the-new-computer-based-structural-exam/).
- Format and cost detail: breadth 55 multiple-choice items (45 scored + 10 pretest) vs 40 on paper; depth scenarios with alternative item types replacing 4 (buildings) or 3 (bridges) constructed-response problems; $350 per section ($1,400 total) vs $500 per component ($1,000) — [SEAOG, "CBT exam" article, Nov 2022, updated Apr 2024](https://seaog.org/articles/CBTexam).
- Josephson's assessment: breadth rates were "slightly higher than past combined scores"; depth rates were lower; and it is hard to know whether depth is genuinely harder because the old compensatory scoring never reported the essay portion separately — [Josephson, STRUCTURE, Sept 2024](https://www.structuremag.org/article/what-in-the-world-is-going-on-with-the-new-computer-based-structural-exam/).
- BPELSG minutes (29 May 2025 meeting): "some boards were concerned with the low pass rates in comparison to pass rates from recent history prior to the transition" — [BPELSG 24 July 2025 meeting materials, containing the May 29 minutes](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20250724_meeting_materials.pdf).

### Inferences
- A candidate now needs to clear a section with a 13-20% first-time pass rate twice (vertical and lateral buildings depth) to finish, where previously the binding constraint was a 26-39% component. Even without knowing the joint probability, completions must have fallen sharply for the April 2024 - October 2025 windows.
- Volumes also changed: buildings component first-time volume was 654 (vertical) and 529 (lateral) in the two paper windows of FY2022-23; CBT buildings depth first-time volume was 342 and 241 in the two windows of FY2024-25. That is roughly half the first-time throughput, though part of the difference is that candidates must now pass (or at least schedule) breadth separately.

### Gaps
- April 2022 and October 2022/April 2023 are only available as fiscal-year pairs in Squared; per-window paper figures were not sought beyond this (earlier exams are researched separately).
- Texas PELS publishes per-administration state results (a search summary quoted April 2023 paper 33% lateral / 27% vertical and October 2023 58% / 24% for Texas); not fetched or verified. See [pels.texas.gov exam stats](https://www.pels.texas.gov/exam_stats/2025/2025-eng-exam-summary.pdf).

## 3. What did NCSEA, SEAOC, SEI/ASCE, STRUCTURE, ENR, state boards say? What were the complaints?

### Takeaway
Documented reaction came from the Structural Engineering Licensure Coalition chair (STRUCTURE, Sept 2024), NCSEA (July 2025 "Career Roadblock"), SEAOI (first SEA to write; May 2025 member notice), SEAOC/NCSEA representatives at BPELSG meetings (April and July 2025), and the Washington, Illinois and California boards. Complaints centred on the test environment (one screen, one reference open at a time, slow PDF references), insufficient time, no partial credit on alternative item types, and scenario quality, rather than the cut score as such.

### Cited Findings
- 4 Sep 2024, STRUCTURE editorial by Carl Josephson, PE, SE (chair, Structural Engineering Licensure Coalition): examinee complaints were that computer-based references were "clunky and slow", only one window could be open, some references lacked bookmarks, candidates could not return to items after the break, and time seemed too short; alternate item types (fill-in-the-blank, multiple-correct, drag-and-drop) give no partial credit. SEI, NCSEA and CASE licensure committees were monitoring and discussing with NCEES, described as "open and receptive". NCEES/Pearson VUE positions reported: no personal references (security), software will not support multiple windows, no plans for multiple monitors — [STRUCTURE, Sept 2024](https://www.structuremag.org/article/what-in-the-world-is-going-on-with-the-new-computer-based-structural-exam/).
- 25 Nov 2024 (video, re-featured by NCSEA): engineer Romulo de Carvalho Silva cites a "shocking passing rate of 16% for the lateral depth" in April 2024 and says he paused his pursuit of the licence after hundreds of hours and dollars — reported in [NCSEA, "Career Roadblock: CBT format of SE Exam", 26 July 2025](https://www.ncsea.com/news-post/read-watch-listen-career-roadblock-computer-based-test-cbt-format-of-se-exam-2/). (Individual testimony; secondary colour.)
- 3 Apr 2025, BPELSG board meeting, public comment by Michael Parolini for SEAOC and NCSEA: the associations "support any pass rate whether it be 0% or 100%, provided that the passers of that exam are minimally competent engineers"; they want the exam "to be more of a test of the content of structural engineering and less of the testing environment"; "it is not a reflection of people's knowledge, it is a reflection [of] getting through a single screen" — [BPELSG minutes, 3 April 2025](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/minutes/20250403_bdmn.pdf).
- 19 May 2025, SEAOI president Jennie Traut-Todaro, SE, told members an extra hour would be added to the depth exams from Spring 2026, first reported by Washington's BRPELS and confirmed by the Illinois Structural Engineering Board; SEAOI noted the change had not been communicated to candidates enrolled for the fall, and urged continued advocacy through state boards — [SEAOI news, 19 May 2025](https://seaoi.org/news/13500834).
- 26 July 2025, NCSEA SE3 Committee column: candidates found breadth manageable and depth very difficult; concern categories listed as exam preparation resources, exam structure and timing, complexity and quality of scenarios, writing tools and surfaces, window management and navigation, ambiguity and length of questions, and quality assurance and scoring. "Multiple state SEAs, beginning with SEAOI" sent feedback to NCEES and state boards; the Washington and Illinois licensing boards "issued a joint memo" asking NCEES to form a task force; NCSEA warned licensure numbers could decline if changes are slow, and urged members to lobby state boards as NCEES's direct clients — [NCSEA, 26 July 2025](https://www.ncsea.com/news-post/read-watch-listen-career-roadblock-computer-based-test-cbt-format-of-se-exam-2/).
- 24 July 2025, BPELSG board meeting: staff (Ric Moore) said the depth portion is the problematic area; candidates lost the ability to bring their own references and "The provided reference materials did not align with candidate expectations". Carl Josephson (for SEAOC) said candidates needed more time regardless of performance — [BPELSG minutes, 24 July 2025](https://bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/minutes/20250724_bdmn.pdf).
- 20 Aug 2025, NCEES Western Zone annual meeting: a structural engineering caucus discussed the exam and "An update regarding CBT PE Structural exam feedback from local structural associations and test takers was provided by most boards" — [NCEES Western Zone 2025 annual meeting minutes](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/WZ-2025-Annual-Mtg-Minutes_FINAL.pdf).
- NCEES's own summary of the feedback it received (Aug 2025): only one screen; only one reference open at a time; examinee time ("speededness"); pass rates — [NCEES 2025 Engineering Forum Presentation](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-Engineering-Forum-Presentation_FINAL.pdf).
- A public commenter for the structural engineers' association asked BPELSG (2025 exam-results review) to report all four structural sections separately because combined rates mislead — search-engine summary of a third-party meeting digest (cpo.soar.com), which could not be fetched; treat as unverified.

### Inferences
- The professional associations deliberately did not ask for a lower cut score; SEAOC/NCSEA's stated position is that any pass rate is acceptable if the exam measures competence, and that the CBT delivery was measuring interface handling. The time-pressure complaint was later conceded by NCEES (see section 4).

### Gaps
- No ENR, ASCE Civil Engineering / Civil Engineering Source, or SEI statement on the pass rates was found in searches; I found no reliable evidence that either outlet covered it.
- The text and date of the SEAOI letter(s) to NCEES, any SEAOC/SEAONC/SEAOSC letter, and the Washington-Illinois joint memo were not found. NCSEA refers to them without dates.
- No published examinee survey results or petition were found (NCSEA's column lists concern categories but no survey numbers).
- Forum colour (not fetched; Reddit mirror returned 403): r/StructuralEngineering threads "October SE exam results" (17 Jan 2025) and "Changes to PE Structural exam coming in 2026" (May 2025); eng-tips thread "SE exam new format April 2024".

## 4. What did NCEES change or announce in response, and did pass rates move?

### Takeaway
NCEES conceded the depth sections were "speeded" and added 60 minutes from April 2026 (announced to boards 28 April 2025); refused a zone-backed motion to shorten the October 2025 exam instead; approved new depth specifications for April 2027 (48 items, 8 pretest, new standard setting); and formed a Structural Practice Task Force for 2025-26. In the first window after the extra hour (April 2026), buildings depth first-time pass rates were 63% (lateral) and 54% (vertical) versus 20% and 13% in the prior fiscal year.

### Cited Findings
- 28 Apr 2025: NCEES memo from Lehmon Dekle, P.E., Chief Officer of Examinations, giving member boards one-year notice that "The total time for PE Structural Engineering Vertical Depth and Lateral Depth sections will become 6.5 hours", adding 60 minutes, effective Spring 2026 — [WA BRPELS, "Structural Exam Changes", 28 Apr 2025](https://brpels.wa.gov/news/2025/structural-exam-changes); restated in [NCEES member board memo, 20 Oct 2025](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Fall-2026-Exam-Changes-Member-Board-Memo.pdf).
- Rationale as relayed to BPELSG: NCEES "met with exam committees, exam volunteers, and psychometricians and determined that the exam was 'speeded'. ... regardless of whether the candidate was high or low performing, all candidates were experiencing a time issue" — May 29, 2025 BPELSG minutes in [BPELSG 24 July 2025 meeting materials](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20250724_meeting_materials.pdf). CONFLICT: those minutes say the added hour would begin "with the October 2025 exam"; the NCEES memo, SEAOI and NCSEA all say April/Spring 2026, and NCSEA states no changes were planned for October 2025.
- 15-17 May 2025, joint Central/Western Zone interim meeting (Albuquerque): the Washington board moved that the NCEES board of directors waive the one-year notice rule so that, from October 2025, "in lieu of adding 60 minutes to the exam time, reduce the number of questions to 48, dropping one scenario". "The California board seconded the motion. After discussion, the motion passed." The Central Zone took a supportive straw poll on cutting items from 60 to 48. The NCEES board of directors "did not adopt a motion to suspend EDP 7 to shorten the October 2025 administration" — zone vice presidents' reports reproduced in [BPELSG 24 July 2025 meeting materials](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20250724_meeting_materials.pdf); Washington's committee noted the board vote in its [16 June 2025 SE committee report](https://brpels.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-06/06-16-25%20SE%20Report.pdf).
- August 2025, NCEES annual meeting Engineering Forum ("PE Structural Exam Update", Lehmon Dekle): depth sections "currently 5.0 hours (exam time) with 5 scenarios, each scenario having a set of 12 questions"; four scenarios have 10 scored + 2 pretest items, and a fifth is a 12-item pretest scenario (40 scored + 20 pretest). Responses to feedback: one screen ("PVUE test center equipment is the same for all clients"); one reference at a time ("a system limitation to mitigate lag due to rendering large PDFs"); time ("60 additional minutes of exam time are being added in April 2026"); pass rates ("The passing standard of competence is established at a standard setting meeting. Equating maintains the same standard, so pass rates are expected to be stable until a new standard of competence is set.") — [NCEES 2025 Engineering Forum Presentation](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-Engineering-Forum-Presentation_FINAL.pdf).
- Same presentation: a PAKS (professional activities and knowledge study) was completed; "EPE formally approved test specifications on August 7, 2025"; new specifications effective April 2027 reduce pretest items from 20 to 8, eliminate the 12-item pretest scenario, raise per-item time "from 5.0 minutes/item today (6.0 minutes/item beginning April 2026) to 7.5 minutes/item", and "A standard setting will be conducted (new standard of competence)" — [NCEES 2025 Engineering Forum Presentation](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-Engineering-Forum-Presentation_FINAL.pdf).
- 20 Oct 2025 memo: Spring 2027, breadth sections get new specifications (question count unchanged) and depth sections get new specifications and "will consist of 48 questions" — [NCEES member board memo, 20 Oct 2025](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Fall-2026-Exam-Changes-Member-Board-Memo.pdf). The NCEES exam page now describes April 2027 depth sections as 48 questions (40 scored, 8 pretest) in a 6.5-hour appointment with 6 hours of exam time, with depth results "available in 10-13 weeks" after "standard setting evaluations" — [NCEES PE Structural exam page](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/structural/).
- NCEES scheduled "a PE Structural exam webinar on October 22, 2025, on the status of the exam and its upcoming changes" — [NCEES Licensure Exchange, October 2025](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Oct-2025-LEx-1.pdf).
- NCEES committee findings published for the August 2026 annual meeting: the Exam Audit Committee's audit of the four depth exams "identified items that fell outside the established metrics for difficulty, discrimination, and/or average examinee response time. It is believed that the transition from constructed-response items to 100 percent alternative item types may be a contributing factor." The committee's charge list includes observing a standard-setting study for "PE Structural: (exam administered April 2026; four depth exams only)". Another committee noted the depth exams from April 2024 "contained a higher proportion of pretest items than other NCEES exams and that the per-item time provided was lower than other NCEES engineering exams" — [2026 NCEES Action Items and Conference Reports](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-NCEES-Action-Items-and-Conference-Reports_web_2.pdf).
- Task force: the 2024-25 Structures Working Group (coordinator Jason Gamble; members included Carl Josephson and Illinois SE Board member Eric Wheeler) recommended "a Structural Task Force be formed for 2025-2026"; President Elizabeth Beckett formed the Structural Practice Task Force, chaired by Chun Lau, P.E., S.E. (Washington), with six charges and six motions for the 2026 annual meeting. Its published charges concern significant-structures definitions, the practice-of-engineering definition and updating state rules that still reference the "16-hour" exam, not exam delivery or pass rates. Separately, the EPE Committee recommended a further task force on whether the two breadth sections alone could support P.E. licensure, naming "Barriers to licensure" as a consideration — [2026 NCEES Action Items and Conference Reports](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-NCEES-Action-Items-and-Conference-Reports_web_2.pdf); [NCEES 2025 Engineering Forum Presentation](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-Engineering-Forum-Presentation_FINAL.pdf).
- Did rates move: first-time depth pass rates in the July 2026 table vs FY2024-25 (Oct 2024 + Apr 2025): Lateral Depth Buildings 63% vs 20%; Vertical Depth Buildings 54% vs 13%; Lateral Depth Bridges 63% vs 48%; Vertical Depth Bridges 37% vs 36%. Repeat takers: 73% vs 21%; 68% vs 16%; 57% vs 38%; 56% vs 60% — [NCEES PE Structural exam page](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/structural/); [NCEES Squared 2025](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-Squared_access.pdf).

### Inferences
- The April 2026 jump coincides with two changes at once: the extra hour (5.0 to 6.0 minutes per item) and, per the Exam Audit Committee charge, a standard-setting study on the April 2026 depth exams. NCEES had said rates would stay "stable until a new standard of competence is set", so a re-set passing standard is the likely main driver, but no source found states what the new standard was or apportions the effect. Present this as an association in time, not a documented cause.
- Whether a new standard setting occurred for April 2026 or is still to come with the April 2027 specifications is ambiguous: the Forum slides tie the new standard setting to April 2027, while the audit committee charge lists a standard-setting study for April 2026.
- Breadth pass rates did not move materially over the whole period (first-time 40-49%), consistent with the problem being specific to the depth format.

### Gaps
- October 2025 depth results (last window before the extra hour) not found, so the before/after comparison skips one window.
- No NCEES news release or Licensure Exchange article explaining the April 2026 results was found. The 22 Oct 2025 webinar content was not found.
- The outcome of the six Structural Practice Task Force motions at the August 2026 annual meeting was not checked (the 2026 minutes were not located).

## 5. How many examinees sat each section per year, and is there evidence of fewer SE licences?

### Takeaway
Volumes are in the Squared tables (section 1). Buildings depth first-time volume per two-window year was 241 (lateral) and 342 (vertical) in FY2024-25, against 529 and 654 first-time paper component takers in FY2022-23. Direct licence-count evidence is thin but consistent: Washington issued 40 SE licences in 2024, 35 of them by comity, and only 1 by exam in Q1 2025; SEAOC told the California board that "70 licenses issued per year is low".

### Cited Findings
- FY2023-24 CBT first-time/repeat volumes: Lateral Breadth 248/23; Lateral Depth Bridges 61/0; Lateral Depth Buildings 198/0; Vertical Breadth 385/19; Vertical Depth Bridges 39/0; Vertical Depth Buildings 281/0. Same year, final paper window (Oct 2023): Lateral Bridges 34/49; Lateral Buildings 271/310; Vertical Bridges 31/33; Vertical Buildings 241/237 — [NCEES Squared 2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Squared-2024_pages.pdf).
- FY2024-25 first-time/repeat volumes: Lateral Breadth 318/105; Lateral Depth Bridges 58/29; Lateral Depth Buildings 241/155; Vertical Breadth 442/100; Vertical Depth Bridges 53/15; Vertical Depth Buildings 342/160 — [NCEES Squared 2025](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-Squared_access.pdf).

### Inferences
- Nationally, at most 112 people passed Lateral Depth Buildings and 111 passed Vertical Depth Buildings across the first three windows (15 months). Since a new SE via the CBT route needs both (plus both breadth sections), the number completing the full buildings exam nationally in that period was at most about 110 and probably well below it. This is the strongest quantitative link between the exam transition and the dashboard's 71 (2024) and 62 (2025) California licences, most of which would have had to come from paper-exam carry-overs, comity, or the small CBT passing pool.
- The SEAOC "70 licenses issued per year is low" remark (July 2025) matches the dashboard's 2024 count of 71, so the profession's own representative tied the low count to the exam discussion at the Board, though the minutes do not record him stating causation.
- Washington's 2024 mix (35 of 40 by comity) suggests exam-route licensure had nearly stopped there; however no pre-2024 Washington baseline was found, so the comity share alone does not prove a change.
- The April 2026 results (first-time passes of roughly 83 in lateral buildings depth and 102 in vertical buildings depth, plus about 118 and 103 repeat passes, computed from the published volumes and rounded percentages) imply a release of the backlog; California SE issuance might be expected to rebound in late 2026 and 2027. This is a projection, not a finding.

## 6. Did the California Board (BPELSG) discuss this or take action?

### Takeaway
Yes. BPELSG discussed the exam at least at its April, May and July 2025 meetings, joined an informal call of SE-licensing boards with NCEES, seconded Washington's May 2025 zone motion to shorten the October 2025 depth exams, published California-vs-national section data in July 2025, and has an emeritus member (Carl Josephson) on the NCEES Structural Practice Task Force. No formal BPELSG motion, letter or rule change on the exam was found.

### Cited Findings
- 3 Apr 2025: Executive Officer Ric Moore reported "an informal online call between him and the boards that license structural engineers, NCEES Exams Chief, Board member Alireza Asgari, and Staff Civil Engineer Natalie King. NCEES is continuing to say that they will continue to do whatever they can to improve the exam". President Wong "expressed an appreciation that California is not the only one experiencing this issue". SEAOC/NCSEA public comment (Parolini) supported staff's further involvement with NCEES — [BPELSG minutes, 3 April 2025](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/minutes/20250403_bdmn.pdf).
- 29 May 2025: Moore reported that "Structural exam administration issues were a huge topic of discussion" at the zone meeting; described NCEES's "speeded" finding and the one-hour extension; and reported that Washington's motion to drop items instead "passed in both the Western and Central Zone sessions" but "was denied" by the NCEES Board of Directors. President Wong "participated in the structural engineering discussion" at the zone engineering forum — May 29 minutes in [BPELSG 24 July 2025 meeting materials](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20250724_meeting_materials.pdf).
- California seconded the Washington motion: "The California board seconded the motion. After discussion, the motion passed." — Western Zone vice president's report in [BPELSG 24 July 2025 meeting materials](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20250724_meeting_materials.pdf).
- 24 July 2025: Moore "provided data from the first three computer-based administrations of the new NCEES PE-Structural exam which has had some controversy due to the change in format"; California candidates' pass rates "tend to exceed the national average"; public comment from Carl Josephson and Michael Parolini (both SEAOC). Emeritus member Carl Josephson was named to the NCEES Structural Task Force — [BPELSG minutes, 24 July 2025](https://bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/minutes/20250724_bdmn.pdf).
- Later meeting (minutes included in the 15 Jan 2026 materials): Josephson reported the Structural Practice Task Force "met for the second time. They have six charges; however, they were advised not to discuss the charges with the Board at this time" — [BPELSG 15 Jan 2026 meeting materials](https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20260115_meeting_materials.pdf).

### Inferences
- BPELSG's posture was to work through NCEES channels (zone motion, informal multi-board call, task-force seat) rather than act unilaterally; California has no state-specific substitute for the NCEES structural exam in this period, so it had no independent lever over the pass rate.
